# Article III — Interruptibility

Interruptibility establishes that humans can pause, redirect, or terminate autonomous flows at any governance-critical boundary.
No process is considered safe if intervention is theoretically possible but practically inaccessible.

## Constitutional intent

This article protects against runaway behavior, cascading policy defects, and operator lockout.
Human override is treated as core infrastructure.

## Operational controls

- Manual and automated pause triggers are tested through recurring drills.
- Rollback choreography is documented with explicit ownership by role.
- Critical paths include timeout-based safe states.
- Operator consoles preserve low-latency access under incident load.

## Stewardship note

Interruptibility keeps sovereignty anchored in human judgment while still enabling high-speed systems.
