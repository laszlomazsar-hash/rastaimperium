# Article VII — Temporal Asymmetry

Temporal Asymmetry prioritizes long-horizon safety and institutional resilience over short-term optimization.
Governance choices are evaluated for second- and third-order effects across time.

## Constitutional intent

This article protects against local decisions that seem beneficial now but weaken future control, trust, or adaptability.
It gives strategic weight to durable architecture and reversible pathways.

## Operational controls

- Long-horizon risk registers are reviewed alongside release plans.
- Architectural decisions include explicit deprecation and migration strategies.
- Incentive systems reward resilience, not only rapid throughput.
- Scenario planning informs policy updates and governance investment.

## Stewardship note

Temporal Asymmetry is the discipline of future accountability.
The constitution must remain governable by successors, not only by current operators.
